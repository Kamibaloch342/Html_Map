1. Provincial Distribution of Persons Engaged in Domestic & Care Work by Sex

Absolute population counts (in Millions) performing unpaid domestic and care work across Pakistan's 4 provinces.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 12.1 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 137)
Exact Survey Text: "In 2024-25, about (117.4) million persons in Pakistan are engaged in domestic and care work, with female (66.7) million and male (50.7) million at national level. Punjab accounts for the largest share with (62.6) million, followed by Sindh (28.0) million, KP (21.0) million, and Balochistan (5.8) million."
Provincial Female Shares: Punjab: 36.5M females (58.3%) vs 26.1M males; Sindh: 15.3M females (54.6%) vs 12.7M males; KP: 11.6M females (55.2%) vs 9.4M males; Balochistan: 3.3M females (56.9%) vs 2.5M males.
Methodological Insight: In every province, female caregivers exceed males. Punjab accounts for 54.7% of all female unpaid caregivers in Pakistan due to its demographic scale.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 12 Table 12.2 (Part A) Report Page 139 (PDF Page 181) 179.6M Working-Age Population

2. Participation Rates Across All 14 Domestic Activities: The Gender Divide (%)

Percentage of female (87.6M) and male (92.0M) working-age population performing each of the 14 discrete unpaid tasks.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 12.2 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 139)
Top Gender Chasm: Cooking, Cleaning & Washing Clothes: Female 59.7% vs Male 6.7% (+53.0 pp gap). Caring for children/elderly: Female 31.1% vs Male 14.6% (+16.5 pp gap). Livestock operations: Female 24.8% vs Male 12.9% (+11.9 pp gap).
Outdoor Activities: Nominally more men participate in fetching water (9.7% male vs 5.4% female) and firewood gathering (10.6% male vs 5.4% female), but female time commitment once engaged is substantially higher (Chart 3).
Shared Tasks: "Other services" (school pick-and-drop, family grocery marketing) shows balanced participation: 25.4% female vs 23.2% male.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 12 Table 12.2 (Part B) Report Page 138–139 (PDF Page 180–181) Hours/Week per Participant

3. Weekly Hours Dedicated per Activity: Female Time Dominance & The "Once-Engaged" Paradox

Mean weekly hours dedicated by active participants in each activity, revealing female time intensity across household and outdoor chores.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 12.2 & Narrative · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Report Page 138)
Core Chores Benchmark: Females spend 15.3 hours/week (2.19 hours/day) on cooking & cleaning compared to only 6.8 hours for males. Livestock maintenance takes 10.1 hours/week for females vs 7.6 hours for males.
The "Once-Engaged" Paradox (Report Page 138 Text): "However, in these activities, female often spend equal or more time than male once they are engaged; for example, female spend more hours fetching water (4.3 vs 3.4) and collecting firewood (7.4 vs 6.9)."
Dependent Care: Helping children do homework requires 8.7 hours/week for females vs 7.6 hours for males. Direct child and elderly care requires 7.5 hours/week for females vs 5.6 hours for males.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 12 Table 12.3 Report Page 139–140 (PDF Page 181–182) Millions of Persons

4. Absolute Population Volume Engaged in Unpaid Labor by Activity (Millions)

Direct headcount comparison of male vs female citizens performing each unpaid domestic and care activity across Pakistan.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 12.3 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 140)
Headcount Comparisons: Cooking & Chores: 52.3 Million Females vs 6.3 Million Males (8.3× asymmetry). Direct Care of Children/Elderly: 27.3 Million Females vs 13.5 Million Males (2.0×). Livestock: 21.7M Females vs 11.9M Males (1.8×).
Total Household Burden: Over 58.6 Million Pakistanis cook and clean, but 89.2% of them are women. This invisible care workforce underpins the entire formal national economy.
Education & Homework: 10.7 Million mothers actively tutor and assist children with schooling compared to 9.7 Million fathers.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 12 Table 12.4 Report Pages 142–143 (PDF Pages 184–185) Provincial Female Weekly Hours

5. Provincial Care Workload Heatmap: Weekly Hours by Province & Activity

Cross-provincial comparison showing how geographical, environmental, and infrastructure differences impact weekly female care hours.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 12.4 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Pages 142–143)
Balochistan Care Drudgery Peak: Balochistan women work the longest hours in Pakistan across nearly all care categories: 15.8 hrs/wk cooking/cleaning, 10.7h livestock, 10.2h childcare, 5.4h fetching water, and 11.6h food preservation.
Punjab Absolute Scale: Punjab women average 16.4 hrs/week on household chores (27.9M women) and 10.5 hrs/week on livestock (11.1M women).
Sindh & KP: KP women average 15.0 hrs/wk on chores and 8.2 hrs on childcare. Sindh women average 12.7 hrs/wk on domestic chores and 9.5 hrs on livestock operations.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 12 Table 12.5 Report Pages 145–146 (PDF Pages 187–188) Hours/Week by Labor Force Status

6. The Unemployment & Inactivity Care Penalty: Care Hours by Labor Force Status

How employment status dictates female care burdens: comparing employed women, unemployed job seekers, and inactive housewives.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 12.5 & Section 12.4 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Report Page 144–146)
The Double Shift for Employed Women: Even with a full-time paid job, employed women perform 14.5 hours/week of domestic chores, 7.6 hours of childcare, and 9.2 hours of livestock operations!
Unemployed Female Surge (Report Page 144): "For female, the effect of unemployment is even more pronounced. Unemployed female spend 17.0 hours per week on cooking/cleaning (vs 14.5 for employed), and 12.0 hours on helping children with homework (vs 8.1 for employed)."
Male Leisure Absorption: When men become unemployed, their domestic chore contribution increases modestly from 6.7 to 9.5 hrs/week, but remains 7.5 hours lower than unemployed women.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 5 Table 5.7 Report Page 77 (PDF Page 112) Mean Monthly Wages (PKR)

7. Average Monthly Wages in Formal, Informal & Agricultural Sectors by Sex (2020–21 vs 2024–25)

Tracking wage growth and gender wage disparities across formal, informal, and agricultural employment sectors in Pakistan.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 5.7 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 77)
2024–25 Monthly Wages: Formal Sector: Rs. 54,038 (Male: 54,451, Female: 51,860). Informal Sector: Rs. 30,834 (Male: 31,066, Female: 28,201). Agriculture: Rs. 22,878 (Male: 24,080, Female: 19,677).
Informal Penalty: Women in the informal economy suffer a -45.6% wage penalty compared to formal female workers. In agriculture, women earn 18.3% less than male farmhands.
5-Year Growth (2020–21 to 2024–25): Overall nominal wages rose from Rs. 24,028 to Rs. 39,042, but rampant consumer inflation has significantly eroded real purchasing power.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 5 Table 5.8 & 5.9 Report Pages 78–79 (PDF Pages 113–114) Annual Income (PKR)

8. Average Annual Income of Self-Employed by Major Industry & Gender (PKR)

Annual entrepreneurial returns for self-employed male vs female own-account workers across major industrial sectors.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 5.8 & Table 5.9 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Report Pages 78–79)
National Self-Employed Totals: Total average annual income: Rs. 589,353 (Male: Rs. 606,427, Female: Rs. 497,120). Self-employed men earn 22.0% more annually than self-employed women.
Urban vs Rural Divergence (Table 5.8): Urban self-employed workers average Rs. 634,944/yr compared to Rs. 564,078/yr in rural areas.
Industry Disparities (Table 5.9): Highest annual income in Manufacturing (Rs. 672,210); Lowest in Transport & Storage (Rs. 514,920).
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 3 Table 3.5 & 3.6 Report Pages 56–58 (PDF Pages 98–100) 1st to 9th Major Occupational Groups

9. The Occupational Crowding Pyramid: Distribution of Employed Women Across 9 Major Occupations (%)

Proportion of employed males vs females across the 9 standard International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO) groups.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 3.6 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 57)
Extreme Crowding in Agriculture: Over 42.1% of all working women in Pakistan are crowded into Group 6 (Skilled Agricultural & Fishery Workers), compared to 30.2% of men.
Craft & Needlework: Group 7 (Craft & Related Trades, including home tailoring) employs 18.5% of women vs 15.4% of men.
Severe Under-Representation in Leadership: Group 1 (Managers) employs only 0.6% of women vs 2.8% of men. Group 8 (Plant/Machine Operators) employs 0.3% of women vs 6.8% of men.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Appendix Indicator 5.5.2.b Report Page 150 (PDF Page 192) Senior & Middle Management Parity

10. SDG Indicator 5.5.2.b: The Leadership Glass Ceiling Across Pakistan's Provinces (Rural vs Urban)

Proportion of females in total employment classified as senior and middle managers across all 4 provinces and urban/rural areas.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Indicator 5.5.2.b · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Report Page 150)
National Executive Benchmark: Women account for just 9.6% of Senior and Middle Management nationally (Male: 90.4%). In urban commercial hubs, this collapses to 6.8%.
Provincial Breakdown: Urban Sindh: 4.5%; Urban Balochistan: 4.5%; Urban Punjab: 7.8%; Urban KP: 13.0%.
Care Chokepoint Link: Long 54+ weekly managerial hours (LFS Page 41) combined with peak caregiving at ages 35–44 systematically chokes the female leadership pipeline.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Appendix Indicator 8.3.1 Report Page 151 (PDF Page 193) Non-Agricultural Informality Rate (%)

11. SDG Indicator 8.3.1: Non-Agricultural Informal Labor Vulnerability by Sex & Province

Percentage of non-agricultural employment classified as informal across Pakistan and its four provinces.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Indicator 8.3.1 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Report Page 151)
National Informality: Non-agricultural informality is 72.1% nationally (Male: 73.0%, Female: 66.3%). In rural non-agriculture, female informality reaches 70.9%.
Rural Sindh Peak: In rural Sindh, female non-agricultural informality reaches an astonishing 82.9% (vs 74.0% for men).
Lack of Social Protection: Informal workers lack pension rights, maternity benefits, overtime pay, and workplace safety protections, trapping care-burdened women in precarity.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 9 Table 9.2 & 9.4 Report Page 105 & 107 (PDF Page 147 & 149) 19th ICLS Unemployment Rates (%)

12. Unemployment Rates by Province and Sex Under 19th ICLS Standards (%)

Provincial female vs male unemployment rates, showing the disproportionate labor market friction faced by Pakistani women.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 9.2 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 105)
National Unemployment: Under 19th ICLS, national unemployment is 7.1%, but female unemployment is 10.5% (1.75× male unemployment of 6.0%).
Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Extreme: KP records the highest female unemployment in Pakistan at 18.1% (vs 7.5% for men; 2.4× gap). Punjab female unemployment is 9.6% vs 6.3%.
Job Search Friction: Mobility constraints and lack of safe female transit prevent educated women from finding jobs matching their skills.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 9 Table 9.13 Report Page 116 (PDF Page 158) Inactive Working-Age Population

13. Structural Reasons for Labor Market Inactivity: The 78.4% Housekeeping Chokehold

Primary reported reasons why working-age men and women are not available for paid employment in Pakistan.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 9.13 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 116)
Housekeeping Dominance: An overwhelming 78.4% of inactive women report "Housekeeping / Family and Care Commitments" as the sole reason for non-availability for paid work (compared to only 1.2% of inactive men).
Education Contrast: 64.2% of inactive men are outside the labor force because they are pursuing education/training (vs only 14.1% of women).
Policy Significance: Female economic inactivity is not voluntary leisure: it is an imposed domestic subsidy that absorbs nearly 8 out of 10 inactive women.
PBS LFS 2024–25 Chapter 8 Table 8.8 Report Page 102 (PDF Page 144) Youth Aged 15–24 Years (SDG 8.6.1)

14. Youth Demographic Trap (SDG 8.6.1): NEET Rates (Ages 15–24) by Sex & Area (%)

Share of young people (15–24 years) Not in Employment, Education or Training (NEET) across rural and urban Pakistan.

🏛️ Official Empirical Citation & Methodology Specification Table 8.8 · PBS LFS 2024–25 (Annual Report, Page 102)
The Youth Gender Divide: Female Youth NEET rate is 56.8% nationally compared to 18.4% for male youth—a staggering 38.4 percentage point gap.
Rural Female Trap: In rural Pakistan, female youth NEET exceeds 61.2%. Upon reaching age 16–18, young women are pulled out of school directly into unpaid sibling care and domestic chores.
Demographic Dividend Loss: Pakistan's youth bulge cannot translate into economic prosperity while more than half of its young female population remains locked out of both classrooms and jobs.
